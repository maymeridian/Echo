Voices

    A voice is one person or character: the lines you practise, the recordings they came from, and your voice score across them.

    Learn a voice, not just a line

    Paste a link to a video of the voice you want to imitate: a YouTuber, a podcast, a compilation of a character's lines.

    1. Echo gets the voice lines: it downloads the video, splits it into lines, works out who's speaking, and picks the best lines of the main voice.
    2. Practise lines one at a time. A coach says what to fix after each take.
    3. Run a voice check: several lines, one take each, averaged into your voice score. One line can be luck; several show the impression holds.

    No video? Type a name instead, then upload a recording or record clips on the voice's page.

    Your voices

    How to practise
    1. Pick a line from a voice. Start with the suggested ones: clear and about 3 seconds long.
    2. Listen two or three times, slowed down if it helps. Play the target's note and hum it.
    3. Record a take. The coach picks the one thing that will lift your score most and gives you a drill for it.
    4. Move on when a line stops improving, and come back to it later.
    5. Run a voice check now and then: it shows whether the impression holds across lines.
